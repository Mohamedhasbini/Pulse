Pulse dashboard: what to improve before launch

What the research says about colours, glass layers, backgrounds and data, what makes clients and investors happy with a dashboard, and what gets bad reviews. Ends with a concrete list of changes to the preview. All your tabs, tools and data stay. Only the look and the way things are arranged change.

The short version

  • Glass is for the frame, not for the numbers. The common advice is to put blur on navigation, panels and pop-ups, and keep charts and tables on nearly solid surfaces. Blur behind numbers makes them harder to read when real data changes underneath.
  • One question per screen, one headline number first. 5–7 numbers per view is the usual ceiling before people stop taking them in. The rest goes one click deeper. This is exactly the Focus + list idea.
  • Colour means something, or it isn't used. Green = good / main, orange = needs attention, violet = AI. Red only when something is broken. Then people learn to trust the colours.
  • Dark mode should not use pure white on pure black. Very high contrast blurs for many eyes (especially with astigmatism) and tires people over a day. Off-white text on dark grey-green is calmer.
  • Small business owners judge by "does this help me today?" The most common complaints about booking software are: too much information, widgets that don't help day-to-day, and weak reports. Not "it's not pretty".
  • Investors judge by the first 10 seconds. Clean, consistent and effortless on the main path. A demo with real-looking data and no empty or broken states.

Colours

Keep: the three Pulse colours at the medium strength you approved (green #34b26f, violet #7f72e6, orange #e07a42 in dark mode).

Add a meaning to each, used the same way on every page:

ColourMeansUsed for
GreenGood / the main numberHeadline card, main chart line, "booked", "answered", growth
OrangeNeeds youHanded off, waiting, approvals, things to do. Never decoration.
VioletThe AIAI reply time, Teach AI, confidence, AI queue
Grey / off-whiteEverything elseComparison lines, last period, axes, secondary bars
Red (rare)BrokenDisconnected channel, failed reminder, error. Nothing else.

Dark mode text: use off-white (about #eef1ef) not #fff. Secondary text at about 70%, labels at about 45%.

Light mode: the green card stays deep green. Charts use the darker versions of the three colours so they still read on white glass.

Layers and background

The layer system from the preview is right. Two adjustments based on the research:

  • Data cards get more solid. Raise the card fill so blur shows only at the edges and in the frame. Number and chart areas sit on 85–90% solid glass. Text is checked at 4.5:1 contrast (body) and 3:1 (big numbers, chart lines) against the real background, not a flat colour.
  • Blur only on 2 layers at most: the top bar / side panel and the page sheet. Cards don't blur again on top. This also keeps phones smooth, since stacked blur is heavy on mid-range Android.
  • Background picture: keep it soft and dim (the veil). Offer 3 options in Settings → Look and feel: Calm room (default), Plain (no picture), and later Your own photo (blurred automatically). Plain is also the automatic choice when the device asks for less transparency.

Data and charts

  • Every list row has a real headline number (64%, 7 today, $1,840). No "View" or "3 items".
  • Every number has context: a small change label ("+18% vs last week") and a short sentence on hover or under the chart ("You booked 3 more than last week").
  • One period switch per page (7 days / 30 days / 12 months) in the same place on every tab.
  • Thin lines, rounded small bars, faint grid, last period as a dotted grey line. That's how the reference charts look calm and expensive.
  • Empty and loading states are designed, not blank. A new business with no data sees a friendly example and what to do next.
  • Numbers line up: monospace digits for values in lists and tables so columns don't jump.

What gets good reviews and bad reviews

Praised

  • "Easy to use", "I see my day at a glance"
  • Fast to learn without training
  • Great support behind a complex tool
  • Clear revenue and booking reports
  • Looks modern and trustworthy in front of staff and clients

Complained about

  • "Too much information to go through" for a busy owner
  • Dashboard widgets that don't help day-to-day work
  • Reports too basic or not customisable
  • Takes time to learn all the features
  • Few options to style the dashboard; slow or failing logins

Based on public reviews of booking tools (Checkfront, BookingPress, Wix Bookings, SimplyBook.me, Booker) on Capterra, G2 and Endorsal. Small sample, indicative only.

What makes a client or investor happy in a demo

  • First 10 seconds: the Today screen says what is happening and what needs you, in plain words, with one strong number.
  • Consistency: same card shapes, same spacing, same colour meaning on every tab. Inconsistent styling reads as "unfinished".
  • An effortless happy path: open → see today → open a conversation → see the AI booked it. No dead ends, no empty boxes.
  • Proof of value on screen: "The AI booked $1,840 for you this month" is the line that sells to both owners and investors.
  • Polish signals: smooth tab changes, no layout jumps, readable on a phone.
