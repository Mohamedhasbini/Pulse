What the references do, and what we take

The numbers and text in this preview are placeholders. Fonts are unchanged (Geist). Only surfaces, background and charts are new.

Kerfa CRM (reel 2 & 3)A blurred photo of a real room sits behind everything, dimmed so it never fights the data. Cards are frosted glass: the room shows through, softened. One warm colour (orange) carries all the data, a dotted line shows "last year", and columns show pace against a target.
  • Take: photo + veil background, frosted cards, dotted comparison line, pace columns, charcoal active tab.
Finance overview (reel 1)Every tile is a different loud gradient (indigo, olive, pink, copper, green). It looks great in a video but is tiring to work in all day. Its chart shapes are excellent: flowing bands, a fine-tick gauge, tick-bar meters, rounded pill bars.
  • Take: the chart shapes only. Leave out: the loud tile colours.
The layer system in this preview
  • 0 · Picture: a soft, blurred room, painted in CSS here. It can be swapped for a real photo per business.
  • 1 · Veil: dark or cream wash that tones the picture down.
  • 2 · Sheet: one big glass panel holding the page.
  • 3 · Cards: lighter glass on the sheet.
  • 4 · Chips / rows: the lightest glass, for table rows, tags and pills.
Each layer is a bit lighter than the one under it, so depth comes from transparency, not shadows or 3D.
Why it is easier on the eyes
  • No bright glows or neon. The picture is blurred and dimmed by about 55%.
  • One data colour plus neutrals. Green and red only for status.
  • Thin lines and soft fills in charts, and the grid is barely visible.
  • The same layers in dark and light, so switching feels calm.
Cost and risks
  • Blur is heavy on weak phones and old laptops. Plan: the full effect on desktop, a lighter blur on phones, and none when "reduce transparency" is on.
  • Text contrast on glass has to be checked on every page. The veil is what keeps it readable.
  • The CSS today is several stacked theme files. This should become one clean theme file, replacing them, not another layer.
